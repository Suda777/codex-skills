# Content Workflow

Use this workflow when deciding what a leadership slide should say.

## Build the narrative

1. Identify the audience and the decision, understanding, or confidence the deck should create.
2. State the deck's main conclusion in one sentence.
3. Arrange slides so each page advances that conclusion instead of repeating a topic label.
4. Give every slide one communication job and one memorable takeaway.

## Filter source material

Place input into four layers:

- **Visible conclusion:** what the leader must notice immediately.
- **Supporting evidence:** current figures, progress, examples, comparisons, or reasons.
- **Speaker support:** useful context that may belong in notes rather than on the canvas.
- **Background only:** information supplied to help Codex understand the situation; do not display it automatically.

Distinguish confirmed current facts, historical facts, user judgments, Codex inferences, and missing information. Prefer an explicit placeholder to an invented value.

## Write leadership-facing copy

- Use a judgment or conclusion as the slide title when appropriate.
- Prefer short, direct sentences over document prose.
- Keep terminology consistent across the deck.
- Explain what a number means and why it matters.
- Remove repeated opening and closing sentences that make the same point.
- Convert generic claims such as "加强协同" into a specific actor, action, object, and expected result when evidence supports them.

## Cover the useful management questions

Use only the questions relevant to the slide or deck:

- Why does this matter now?
- What has been achieved?
- What business or customer value resulted?
- What problem or constraint remains?
- What will happen next?
- What decision, resource, or support is requested?

Do not force all six questions onto every slide.
