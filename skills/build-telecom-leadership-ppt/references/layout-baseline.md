# Layout Baseline

Use this only when the user has not supplied an approved reference deck, screenshot, or layout example. Treat it as a temporary baseline that can be refined when the user provides examples.

## Choose layout from the content relationship

- **One conclusion with several reasons:** dominant conclusion plus two to four supporting points.
- **Current state, problem, and recommendation:** directional left-to-right or top-to-bottom progression.
- **Progress by time:** timeline only when sequence is the actual message.
- **Progress by district, project, or workstream:** aligned matrix with consistent fields and a visible status hierarchy.
- **Before and after or competitor comparison:** balanced comparison with a clear contrast dimension.
- **Strategy or next steps:** goal, actions, expected result; use sequence only when dependencies are real.
- **One dominant metric:** large metric with a short interpretation and restrained supporting evidence.

Do not force unrelated content into equal cards. Avoid a dashboard look unless the information is genuinely dashboard-like.

## Establish hierarchy

- Make the page conclusion the strongest textual element after the deck title.
- Use size, weight, color, and position together; do not rely on color alone.
- Keep no more than three meaningful hierarchy levels on a slide.
- Balance density across the canvas; avoid both crowded corners and unexplained empty areas.
- Shorten copy or change the composition before shrinking text.
- Ensure emphasized figures and terms remain understandable in context.

## Maintain deck consistency

- Inherit the supplied deck's theme, typography, margins, title position, and emphasis system.
- Keep recurring page elements stable.
- Vary composition only when the content relationship changes.
- Use one primary color and one restrained accent when no reference exists.
- Do not hard-code red-white, blue-white, a logo, or a single card style as a permanent preference.

## Preview faithfully

Render previews from editable slide sources. Inspect the full-size image for wrapping, uneven spacing, weak emphasis, misalignment, and unintended style drift. Do not approve a layout based only on a contact sheet.
